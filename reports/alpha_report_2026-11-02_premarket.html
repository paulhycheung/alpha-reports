1. Marvell (MRVL) / Lumentum (LITE): $115B all-stock merger. MRVL −3.6% (SMW; +0.6% in an earlier snapshot), LITE −6.8%

Thesis: This is the largest AI-hardware deal of the cycle, announced on the first trading day after the AI rout. Both stocks are falling pre-market, which says the market sees Marvell overpaying in stock at a time when AI multiples are under pressure. The tell is the deal spread. At the SMW snapshot, 4.5 × $189.28 = ~$852 of implied value per LITE share, vs. LITE at $783.80, a gap of ~8.7%. By the same math the gap was ~5% at Friday's close. A wider spread means the market is pricing in more completion or financing-value risk: the close is not expected until H1 2028, and both shareholder votes plus antitrust approvals are required. Watch two things at the open: whether the acquirer keeps sliding (Marvell holders will own ~67% of the combined company), and how Broadcom trades, since the deal builds a larger rival in AI optical and connectivity.

Evidence: Marvell press release / Business Wire (exchange ratio 4.500, ~$1,132 per LITE share or a 23.9% premium to the Oct. 28 unaffected close, ~67/33 ownership, >$1B cost synergies, accretive to non-GAAP EPS in year one, TAM $550B). CoinCentral (Friday close $196.40, −10.05%; −22% since talks; Nvidia invested $2B in each company this year). SMW pre-market quotes. Investing.com (LITE FQ1 revenue $1.26B).

Risks: The implied value moves with MRVL every tick, so the spread can swing quickly. A broader chip rally could lift both. Regulatory review, including China, could stretch the timeline. Merger-arb selling and hedging can put mechanical pressure on MRVL that has nothing to do with fundamentals.
