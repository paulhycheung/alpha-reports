1. Overnight & Global Summary

The "AI users vs. AI builders" split from Wednesday went global overnight. Asian chip and AI-infrastructure stocks were hit hard on fears that a Democratic Congress, plus state-level data-center moratoria, will slow the build-out. South Korea's Kospi fell 5.3% to 5,383.80, with SK Hynix −9.4% and Samsung −7.6%. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.8% to 61,077.90, led by Advantest −15%, Tokyo Electron −9.5% and SoftBank −6.9%. Taiwan's Taiex fell 3.2%. Markets with little AI exposure rallied: Hang Seng +3.7% to 24,796.98, ASX 200 +2.6%, India's Sensex +2.8%, Topix +1.2% (AP; TipRanks). Swissquote's Ipek Ozkardeskaya framed the split: Europe and China are "surfing on hopes" of tariff relief, while for AI the Democratic win is "a potential obstacle to an investment story already facing questions over massive spending, future returns and financing" (AP).

Europe opened higher: DAX +0.6% (26,598), CAC 40 +1.2% (8,277), FTSE 100 +1.0% (10,935, its highest since Aug. 7). The Bank of England was expected to raise rates 25 bp to 4.0% today, its first hike in more than three years (TipRanks); the outcome was not captured.

US futures are split, with the same rotation as Wednesday. Yahoo's strip: Dow futures 53,886 (+0.72%), S&P 500 futures 7,733 (+0.17%), Russell 2000 futures +0.75%, Nasdaq-100 futures 28,421 (−0.51%). Other snapshots fall in the same range (Dow +0.4% to +0.7%, S&P −0.1% to +0.2%, NDX −0.5% to −1.0%). By sector ETF, communication services (XLC +1.49%) and financials (XLF +1.27%) lead, while tech (XLK −0.86%) lags (Current Logic). A Bloomberg headline says a semiconductor ETF is down more than 3% in pre-market. The 10-year yield is ~4.89% (−6 bp) and the VIX ~16.8. Oil is choppy after Wednesday's ~8–9% plunge: Reuters had a fourth day of losses in Asia (WTI $86.92 at 04:26 GMT), but by the European morning Brent was back at ~$95–96 (+1–1.8%) and WTI ~$88–89. Vice President JD Vance's post that "the reason we lost… is the war" fed hopes of an Iran deal (Yahoo/Reuters). Gold rose ~1.7% to ~$4,130–4,144, and Bitcoin is back above $80K.

Tone: constructive for the broad market and cyclicals, defensive for semis and AI infrastructure. The question for the open is whether day-2 chip selling (Qualcomm's guide, the Asian rout) drags the Nasdaq enough to offset the Dow's continued bid.
