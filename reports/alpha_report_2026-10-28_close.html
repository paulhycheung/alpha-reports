1. How the Full Session Traded

Stocks closed modestly higher in a narrow stock-picker's session, and the bigger news arrived after the bell, when the hyperscalers split. The S&P 500 rose +0.28% to 7,744.28, the Nasdaq Composite +0.27% to 26,549.85, the Dow +0.19% to 52,478.91 and the Russell 2000 +0.15% to 2,823.00 (Yahoo Finance; CNBC prints the S&P at 7,743.98 and the Dow at 52,475.10). CNBC's headline calls it a third straight gain for the S&P 500 and the Dow. The Fed held rates at 3.75%–4.00% in a hawkish 10–2 vote, with Cook and Hammack dissenting for a hike. That briefly erased the S&P's gain around 2:15 PM, but the S&P 500 closed at its highest level among today's captured checks. Bonds absorbed the message: the 10-year closed at 5.05% (+5 bp). Futures price about an 85% chance of a hike on Dec. 9 and a policy rate near 4.7% by September 2027, roughly three more hikes (Bloomberg data via Yahoo). The VIX fell to 16.88 (−3.7%). December WTI rose 2.7% to $102.58, gold ended about flat at ~$4,078 and bitcoin gained 1.3% to ~$81,700.

Leadership stayed narrow all day: memory chips (SK hynix ADR +13.3%, Micron "almost 6%"), Oracle (+10.6% after its Investor Day) and AI power (Vertiv +15.6% to +17.3%). Several mega-caps lagged: Nvidia, Meta, Tesla and Apple were each down ~0.7–1.3% in the last captured snapshots. Earnings dispersion was extreme. F5 (≈−20%), Enphase (−17%) and Landstar (−17%) fell, while Genmab (+30% to +33%), Axogen (+31%) and OrthoPediatrics (+31%) rose. After the bell: Microsoft rose ~5% in extended trading after Azure grew 48%. Alphabet fell in early after-hours trade, by ~5–7% per live blogs, after EPS came in far below consensus and free cash flow was negative, even though Cloud revenue roughly doubled. Tone: risk-on in AI hardware and Oracle, hawkish on rates, and a two-sided setup for tomorrow's open.
