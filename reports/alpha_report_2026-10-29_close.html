1. Amazon (AMZN): AWS accelerated, but the stock is being judged on cash flow

Move: −4.25% to $243.79 in the regular session (59.6M shares, ~1.5× average). After hours it traded at $231.52 (−5.0%) at 4:35 PM, −9.1% vs. Wednesday's close (Yahoo). Reuters cites −6.5% and GeekWire more than −7%.

Thesis: This is the third hyperscaler test in two days, and it fails the bar Alphabet set: growth is not enough without free cash flow. AWS accelerated to +39% ($46.0B), its fastest in nearly five years, with a $578B backlog. Jassy guided to AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028. But EPS of $1.89 missed $1.97, Q4 sales guidance of $240–247B straddles a $244.5B consensus, and Q4 operating income guidance of $28.5–33.5B has a midpoint below the ~$33B estimate. Free cash flow was negative on a $94.8B y/y jump in capex purchases. 2026 cash capex stays at ~$220B, with "meaningfully more" in 2027 (Yahoo; GeekWire; Investing.com; Benzinga transcript via search summary). Tomorrow's question is whether the market treats Amazon like Alphabet (−12.6%) or like Microsoft (−0.2%). The AWS acceleration gives it a stronger case for the Microsoft treatment than Alphabet had. The read-through targets are AVGO (already ~−3% AH), NVDA, META and the power/infrastructure trade. Technically, the regular-session close sat just above the ~$243.39 200-day average (Afternoon Edition), so the after-hours level is below it.

Evidence: Yahoo Amazon live blog (close/AH quote, Q4 guides, AWS, Jassy quotes); GeekWire and Investing.com via search summary (AWS, backlog, capex, FCF); Reuters close report (−6.5% AH).

Risks: The conference call (5 PM ET) can swing the AH print either way, especially any 2027 capex number or AWS margin detail. A strong AWS narrative could turn a gap-down into a buyable-dip debate by midday Friday. Alternatively, a second consecutive hyperscaler drop could pull the Nasdaq lower and end the rotation's "money stays in the market" framing.

Confidence: Medium that AMZN opens lower; Low on whether the gap holds through Friday

2. Intel (INTC): a beat with a guide that breaks the margin story

Move: −3.62% to $92.07 into the print (down ~20% over the past month), then −16% to −20% after hours (24/7 Wall St.; Investing.com headline; CoinDesk via search summary).

Thesis: The quarter was strong: adjusted EPS $0.52 vs. $0.40, revenue $16.51B, Data Center & AI +78% to $7.32B (fastest in 15 years), foundry revenue +58%, gross margin 44.1%. The Q4 guide undercut it: revenue $15.0–16.0B vs. ~$17.05B expected, EPS $0.25 vs. $0.43, and gross margin stepping down to 38.0%. Management also flagged a PC slowdown through at least the first half of 2027 and continued Xeon supply limits (24/7 Wall St.; SEC filing link therein). The AH move is roughly double the options-implied ~9% (BigGo via search summary), with ATM implied vol at 154.7% for Friday expiry. Friday tests whether the stock's 150% YTD gain unwinds further. It also matters for read-through to AMD (reports Nov. 3), and for whether server-CPU supply shortages are seen as Intel-specific or an industry constraint.

Evidence: 24/7 Wall St. Intel live blog (results, guide, bull/bear case, options positioning, close quote); Benzinga and CoinDesk via search summary (AH moves).

Risks: AH moves on extreme implied vol often overshoot. The call commentary on 18A yields or external foundry customers could narrow the drop. Heavy call open interest at $95–96 is now far out of the money, which can add to Friday-expiry selling.

Confidence: Medium on a large gap down; Low on day-two direction

3. Alphabet (GOOGL): the record is set; day two meets Amazon

Move: −12.64% to $283.82, the worst session since its 2004 IPO, on 134.5M shares. The intraday range was $280.52–$292.42 (Midday Edition), and it closed in the lower third. The market-value loss was roughly $500B+; reports cite $511–524B at various points (Benzinga; FinanceFeeds via search summary), still short of Nvidia's $593B record.

Thesis: The morning gap never filled. The stock stayed in a ~$12 range from late morning and closed near the middle of it, so neither a capitulation low nor a reversal is evident. Tonight's Amazon reaction reinforces the narrative that drove Alphabet's drop: AI spending that pushes free cash flow negative gets punished regardless of cloud growth. That raises the odds of a second down day rather than a reflex bounce, though the scale of the reset (EPS $2.12 vs. ~$2.97–3.02; FCF −$9.07B; Q3 capex $51.4B) may also draw value buyers. Watch $280.52 (Thursday's low) and $274.75 (the level at which the cumulative loss would pass Nvidia's record) as reference points.

Evidence: Reuters close (−12.6%); Investing.com volume table; Yahoo live blog (record and Nvidia threshold); Investing.com close story (ad growth slowest in five quarters, capex).

Risks: A sharp AMZN reversal on the call could lift the whole hyperscaler group. Index and ETF flows from XLC (heavily weighted to the two share classes) can exaggerate day-two moves in either direction.

Confidence: Low (direction); Medium that it remains the most-watched mega-cap on Friday

5. Memory (MU, SNDK): SanDisk's guide is the overnight read for Micron

Move: MU −4.98% ($934.27), extending from −3.0% pre-market and −4.6% at midday. SNDK −5.32% ($1,330.05) into its print, down ~21% over the past month. Samsung fell 4% in Seoul on a chip-profit miss (Investing.com).

Thesis: SanDisk delivered a double beat: EPS $49.51 vs. $46.27, revenue $11.03B vs. $10.68B, data center revenue +61% q/q to $4.80B and an 85% GAAP gross margin. It guided the December quarter to $12.2–12.6B, another double-digit step up, and announced an 8-for-1 split effective Nov. 19 (24/7 Wall St.; SEC filing link therein). The early AH reaction was mixed, −4% at first and later reported as +2%. The question is whether the market reads NAND pricing power as intact. If SNDK holds green Friday, MU's two-day slide has a fundamental counterpoint; if SNDK sells off on a beat-and-raise, it signals positioning, not fundamentals, is driving memory.

Evidence: 24/7 Wall St. SanDisk live blog (results, guide, close, technical levels $1,276.67 / $1,424.48); Investing.com quote table (MU close and volume); Reuters (SOX −1.4%).

Risks: Consumer revenue fell 39% y/y, so edge/consumer weakness is real. Thin AH trade makes the initial reaction unreliable. A risk-off Nasdaq open after AMZN and INTC could swamp stock-specific news.

Confidence: Low
