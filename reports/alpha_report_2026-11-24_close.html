1. How the Session Traded

A split session, and the indexes closed well off their lows after a late rally. The S&P 500 closed −0.49% at 7,201.98 (−35.19 pts). The Nasdaq Composite closed −1.49% at 23,730.37 (−359.76). The Dow closed +0.85% at 50,016.49 (+421.18), back above 50,000. All three improved sharply in the last 30 minutes. At 3:29 PM, Yahoo's quote strip showed the S&P at −0.85%, the Nasdaq at −1.98% and the Dow at +0.60%, with NVDA at −7.46% and AMD at −9.45%. The S&P therefore recovered about 0.36 percentage points into the bell, and finished just above the 7,150–7,153 floor watched in earlier editions. Over the day, the S&P went from −1.3% at the open to −1.2% at 11:36, −0.31% at 12:44, −0.85% at about 3:08–3:29 and −0.49% at the close.

Chips, AI hardware and neoclouds were sold. Retail, staples and health care were bought. Kiplinger has NVDA at −5.9% and AMD at −8.9% at the close. Micron fell more than 10% (TipRanks), and HPE plunged 16.3% despite a 50% revenue jump, because its FQ1 outlook and Cloud & AI margin guide disappointed. Analog Devices, CoreWeave, SanDisk, Dell and Alphabet were among the worst performers (TipRanks). On the other side, Abercrombie closed +19.8%, Dick's +14.6% and Nike +5.9%, the best stock in the Dow (Kiplinger). Dollar Tree, Costco, J&J and Moderna also outperformed (TipRanks). Interactive Brokers' José Torres blamed "a renewal of AI safety worries and tariff concerns": the WSJ reported that the administration is preparing higher tariffs on China, the EU and others. Anthropic (ANT) closed below its $83 IPO price for the first time (Kiplinger; context only).

Macro: Conference Board consumer confidence jumped to 103.3 (vs. 92.1 expected), its highest since January 2025. The 10-year yield was about 4.90%, and fed-funds futures still price about 95% odds of a 25 bp hike on Dec. 9 (Yahoo). Late quotes: VIX ~27, WTI ~$81.6, gold ~$3,556 (−2.6%), Bitcoin ~$59.4K. After the bell: Dell posted a very large beat and raised its outlook, and its shares rose after hours. Autodesk (−3.7%), Nutanix (−8.4%) and Workday (−2.8%) fell after hours despite beats (see §4).
