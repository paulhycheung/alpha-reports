Tone: This looks like a fragile, tech-led relief bounce going into a jobs report with downside risk. The open will likely be decided by two things: the payrolls number, and whether systematic selling (vol-control and CTA funds; Goldman sees about $7.5B of S&P for sale over the next week) overwhelms dip buyers. Goldman's Rich Privorotsky leans toward "1998-style liquidity crisis" rather than "March 2000." BTIG and others warn that Thursday may be "just the opening act."
