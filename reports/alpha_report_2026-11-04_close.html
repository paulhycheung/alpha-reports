1. How the Full Session Traded

A broad relief rally, led by the Dow and mega-cap platforms. Semis and the AI build-out names sold off, and tech faded slightly into the bell. The Dow closed at 53,356.12, up 1,569.40 points (+3.03%). That is its best day since April 9, 2025, and it is back above 53,000 for the first time since Sept. 8 (CNBC). The S&P 500 closed at 7,688.62 (+1.58%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 25,736.51 (+1.44%). The Nasdaq-100 gained only about +0.9% to +1.0% (~28,460–28,480), and the Russell 2000 rose ~+1.6% (Trading Economics). Compared with the afternoon edition (~3:10 PM), the S&P slipped from +1.70% and the Nasdaq from ~+1.67%, while the Dow held about +3.0%. NYSE advancers beat decliners 2,098 to 705 (Investing.com).

Macro: Crude finished the regular session sharply lower but bounced off its lows after the afternoon edition. Trading Economics' post-close quote had WTI at ~$90.1 (−6.2%), up from ~$88.50 at 3:10 PM. Brent was ~$96.1 (−7.2%) and gasoline hit a four-month low. The 10-year yield was ~4.91% (about −6 bp), and the VIX ~16.6. Gold rose to ~$4,052 (+3.1%), silver +4.5% and bitcoin ~$80.5K (+4.2%). The dollar index was ~101.6.

After the bell: Qualcomm beat but guided EPS light, and its stock went from ~+1.8% to ~−2% after hours. Arm beat on revenue but missed on royalties, after a −4.6% regular session, and was ~−2% after hours. AppLovin jumped ~8–16% on a double beat. MercadoLibre fell ~8% on an EPS miss. Tone: election clarity and gridlock were read as good for cash-rich incumbents, cyclicals and rate-sensitive names. They were read as bad for the companies that build, power and finance AI capacity. Reuters' framing: a slower build-out "could ease cash demands on tech companies, while pressuring operators and suppliers."

4. Closing Alpha Candidates / What to Watch Tomorrow

Research observations only. These are names worth investigating further going into Thursday, not recommendations to buy or sell.

1. Qualcomm (QCOM): a beat with "mixed" guidance. After hours it went from +1.8% to ~−2% after a flat regular session

Thesis: This is the most important read for tomorrow's chip tape. Fiscal Q4 revenue was ~$10.4B vs. ~$10.1B expected, and adjusted EPS was $2.17 vs. $2.16. Handsets were ~$5.1B, about half the total. The December-quarter outlook splits two ways. Revenue guidance of $10.4–11.2B (midpoint ~$10.8B) is above the ~$10.5B consensus. But adjusted EPS guidance of $2.15–2.35 (midpoint $2.25) is below estimates in the high $2.20s to low $2.30s. Full-year capex of ~$2.5B also compares with ~$1.9B expected (investingLive). Higher sales with lower earnings points to margin or cost pressure, possibly memory costs, which pre-print previews flagged. That is our inference. Secondary coverage also says management expects non-handset revenue to fully replace Apple revenue in FY27, and two hyperscaler custom-chip programs to start generating revenue this quarter. These points are unverified against the release. The stock went into the print having given up its midday bid (+2.3% → ~flat) and was down ~9% over the past week. That lowered the bar. It also explains why the first after-hours reaction was positive before it flipped.

Evidence: Seeking Alpha (4:03 PM: +1.8% after hours, adj. EPS $2.17, "mixed guidance"). investingLive (4:12 PM: revenue, EPS, handset revenue, guidance ranges, capex, ~−2% after hours). Yahoo Finance headline via search ("tops Q4 estimates, offers better-than-anticipated outlook"). AP headline via investingLive ($10.44B revenue). Afternoon edition (pre-print positioning, options implied ±8–11%).

Risks / what could change the picture: The call (4:45 PM ET) could reframe the EPS guide, for example as a one-time cost or a mix shift to data center. Sources disagree on the after-hours move (−2% vs. >−4%). Qualcomm's last four post-earnings moves were small (−2.6% to +0.8%). Arm's royalty miss (candidate 2) adds a handset-weakness overlay. Asian handset suppliers will trade first and set the tone.

Confidence: Medium (guidance mix dominates the reaction tomorrow). Low (direction at the open)

2. Arm (ARM): licensing beat masks a royalty miss. −4.62% regular session to $193.40, then ~−2% after hours

Thesis: Revenue was a record $1.44B (+27% y/y) vs. the ~$1.38B LSEG consensus, above the top of Arm's own $1.33–1.43B range. But the beat came from licensing ($773M vs. ~$625M est.), which is lumpy. Royalties, the recurring, volume-driven line, rose only 8% to $670M vs. ~$721M expected, although data-center royalties reportedly doubled y/y. Q3 guidance is revenue of ~$1.54B (vs. $1.53B) and adjusted EPS of $0.52–0.60 (vs. $0.55), roughly in line. The stock closed below the ~$195 support level that pre-earnings technical coverage flagged, and it is down more than 50% from its June peak near $440. Combined with Qualcomm's handset-heavy quarter, the royalty miss raises the question of whether smartphone unit or pricing weakness is broadening. That is worth checking against Asian handset and foundry names overnight.

Evidence: Search summaries of Reuters via Investing.com and Arm's newsroom (revenue, licensing and royalty vs. estimates, Q3 guide, ~−2% after hours). Seeking Alpha headline ("slips even as Q2 results, guidance top estimates", −1.8% after hours). Trading Economics (regular-session close $193.40, −4.62%). TradingKey preview ($195 support).

Risks / what could change the picture: The 5:00 PM ET call could emphasize AI/data-center royalty growth and AGI-CPU demand, which bulls have been focused on. The after-hours move is modest, so much of the bad news may already be in today's −4.6%. The royalty consensus figures vary by source ($712–721M).

Confidence: Medium (royalty miss is the focus). Low–Medium (follow-through tomorrow)

3. Semis and the AI build-out complex close at the lows. Day 2 is the test: MU −7.05%, NVDA −2.2% to −3.2%, AMAT −4.1%, VST −13.7%, ORCL −1.4% to −1.85%

Thesis: Every edition today flagged that the market was separating AI users (platforms) from AI builders (chips, memory, power, neoclouds). That held through the close, and the chip group did not recover going into the Arm and Qualcomm prints. Micron closed at $794.82, its lowest print of the day. Nvidia was ~$200–202, around a $5T market cap. Applied Materials and Analog Devices were each down ~4%. The policy driver also has a new data point after hours: CBS (via an AP/investingLive headline) reports that 37 anti-data-center ballot measures passed, putting 800–1,000 planned builds at risk. Texas's new governor has pledged a data-center pause, and Texas holds ~27% of US data-center power capacity (Wells Fargo via CNBC). Tomorrow has direct tests of the theme. Core Scientific reports before the open. IREN, Lumentum and Applied Optoelectronics report after the close. Worth investigating: whether the hardest-hit names (VST, CRWV, CIFR, IREN) see short-covering, as VST's late bounce from −15% to −13.7% hinted, or whether day-one selling extends into suppliers.

Evidence: Trading Economics (MU, NVDA, AVGO, AMAT, ADI, ARM closes). Investing.com close wrap (NVDA, VST). Reuters close wrap via rallies.ai (IREN, Hut 8, Applied Digital, Core Scientific "tumbled"; AMD dropped despite its 2027 guide). StockTitan live blog (Texas, Wells Fargo 27% estimate via CNBC). investingLive Livebytes (CBS: 37 measures). Earnings Whispers Nov. 5 calendar.

Risks / what could change the picture: Ballot measures and a governor's pledge are not federal law. Reuters notes analysts see slim odds of federal legislation that would seriously undermine the build-out. One-day drops of 10–20% often bring a reflex bounce. NVDA's close differs by ~1 pt between sources. A strong Core Scientific or IREN backlog update could flip the narrative quickly.

Confidence: Medium (elevated volatility in the group tomorrow). Low (direction)

Thesis: The market read "less build-out" as good for incumbents with existing capacity and free cash flow. The ranking held to the close: Microsoft first, Meta last. Apple and Amazon gave back part of their midday gains (AAPL from +6.7%, AMZN from +7.7%), so the late fade in the Nasdaq came partly from these names. The open question for tomorrow: can 5–7% one-day moves in $3–5T companies with no company-specific news hold for a second session? Or do they partly retrace as the policy-relief trade is digested, with Arm and Qualcomm adding pressure on tech sentiment?

Evidence: Trading Economics close summary and quote tables. Investing.com close wrap (MSFT and AMZN as the top Dow performers after 3M). Earlier editions (intraday path, META bond).

Risks / what could change the picture: The two sources differ by up to ~1 pt on AMZN and AAPL closes. A risk-off move in chips after Arm and Qualcomm could spill into the platforms. Conversely, if the "capex discipline" narrative gains traction, the platform/builder spread could widen further.

Confidence: High (all five closed up). Low–Medium (gains hold on day 2)

6. AppLovin (APP): Q3 double beat, after hours +8% to +16% (~$315), after rallying >10% into the print

Thesis: This is the most notable after-hours gainer among large caps, and a counterpoint to today's software weakness (DDOG, FIS). Q3 revenue was $2.185B (+55% y/y) vs. ~$2.075–2.08B expected and the company's own $2.06–2.09B guide. EPS was $4.30 vs. ~$4.03–4.07. Net income rose 72% to $1.44B, free cash flow was $1.4B, and the company bought back $1.4B of stock in the quarter. The stock was reportedly down ~59% YTD before the print, and the Q2 report had triggered a sell-off. The set-up was therefore "washed out" going in. The thing to investigate is whether the outlook, described as strong by Benzinga but not verified here, is enough to sustain a gap above $300.

Evidence: AppLovin Q3 release via StockTitan (revenue, net income, EPS, FCF, buyback). Benzinga (estimates; +15.9% after hours at ~$315). 24/7 Wall St. live blog (+8%; pre-print rally; FactSet consensus). Benzinga options preview (implied move ~16.5%).

Risks / what could change the picture: After-hours prints ranged widely (+8% to +16%). The options market priced a ~16.5% move, so the reaction may only match expectations. Guidance details and the call could shift sentiment. A 10%+ pre-print run-up raises the risk of "sell the news" at the open.

Confidence: Medium (opens higher). Low (holds the full gap)
