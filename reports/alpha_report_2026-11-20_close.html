1. Session Summary

The selling sped up in the final hour, and Friday ended as the worst day of the sell-off. At the close, the S&P 500 was at 6,910.39 (−3.70%), the Nasdaq Composite at 22,617.73 (−5.28%), the Dow at 48,147.33 (−1.93%) and the Russell 2000 at 2,587.57 (−2.54%). That compares with roughly −2.1% for the S&P and −3.4% for the Nasdaq at Reuters' 2:28 PM snapshot. The S&P 500 and Dow both closed in correction: the S&P is about 10.7% below its Oct 6 record close of 7,818.93, and the Dow about 10.8% below its August record. The S&P posted its seventh straight loss, the longest losing streak since early 2020. The Nasdaq Composite is now negative for 2026. The Nasdaq-100 was about 19% below its record, close to a bear market. Yahoo counted 71 of its 101 members in bear-market territory, including all 20 of its chip and memory names.

Cross-asset: The VIX closed at 38.40 (+34.9%) after briefly touching 40, its highest level since April 2025. Treasuries rallied: the 10-year yield fell about 13–14 bp to 4.91% and the 5-year fell 16 bp to 4.70%. Gold fell 4.5% to $3,518.70 and Bitcoin fell 5.2% to about $58,300, so even havens and crypto were sold as investors raised cash. WTI rose 1.7% to $83.86. Traders scaled back bets on a 50 bp Fed hike in December: CME FedWatch had about 92% odds of a 25 bp hike and about 8% odds of a hold, up from essentially none on Thursday. That came even after Governor Cook said waiting "risks getting behind the curve."

Tone: broad, forced de-risking led by AI hardware. TECHi's basket of ten AI hardware stocks fell 11.4% on the day and 19.5% on the week, its worst week in a series that starts in March 2025, and all ten fell. The hyperscalers that buy the hardware fell much less, averaging −3.9%. Outside tech, some defensives held up: General Mills was +4.7% and Lennar +3.1% at about 1:44 PM (AP). University of Michigan sentiment rose to 51.5 from 48. Dan Ives' list of seven drivers (via Yahoo) included political worries about data-centre build-outs after the midterms, Jensen Huang's exit, China and regulatory risk across semis, fund P&L pain and a hawkish Fed.

1. Nvidia (NVDA): −14.0% to $125.53, +1.2% after hours  Confidence: Medium

Why it matters next week: NVDA now faces a disclosure test with a fixed deadline. Its fiscal Q3 ended Oct 25, so the 10-Q is due within 40 days, by about Dec 4. Nvidia broke its practice of filing the 10-Q on earnings day and said only that it would file "in the coming weeks" (MarketWatch). The only earlier example MarketWatch found of a delay this meaningful was 2006, when Nvidia restated earnings over option grants. Friday's Reuters report that SEC enforcement staff are examining accounting matters makes that filing the key document: watch for changes to risk factors or legal proceedings, any disclosure of a material weakness in internal controls, or another delay.
