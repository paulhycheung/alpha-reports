1. Broadcom (AVGO): a $63 billion financing vehicle for Anthropic's chips, plus an upgrade
Pre-market: +2.2% to +2.4% (≈ $336.71 at ~7 AM vs. a $329.33 close), on top of Monday's +2.38%.
Thesis: Two catalysts stack. First, BofA, Blackstone, Citi and Morgan Stanley announced a $63B special-purpose vehicle that will buy Broadcom's custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and networking equipment and lease them to Anthropic, with deliveries starting in 2027. Anthropic's S-1 describes a five-year equipment lease with ~$125.2B of payments (excluding interest), plus the option to issue up to $42B of convertible notes to Broadcom or a financing partner. Second, D.A. Davidson upgraded the stock to Buy from Neutral and raised its target to $400 from $350. The firm argues the shares price in "near zero" odds that both Anthropic and OpenAI go public. The financing turns a large slice of Anthropic's compute plan into funded, multi-year demand for Broadcom. The open tests whether investors reward that visibility or focus on the strings attached.
Evidence: Quartz and TipRanks (structure, XPUs, 2027 deliveries); Stocktwits (the D.A. Davidson call via TheFly, the S-1 lease and note figures, and the WSJ report that CEO Hock Tan stepped in to close the deal); Reuters via Investing.com (search summary): the first-lien piece is ~$43.5B (69%) and Blackstone is the sole second-lien lender; ZeroHedge and AP (+2.4%).
Risks: Broadcom provides residual-value support equal to the first-lien amount (~$43.5B per Reuters' sources), so it keeps exposure if the chips lose value. The WSJ says some lenders nearly walked away and Tan agreed to higher loan rates, a sign of how tight AI credit has become. Worries about "circular" financing, where suppliers fund their own customers, are growing. The stock is still down ~5% this year vs. +29% for XLK (Stocktwits), and hyperscaler capex commentary on Wednesday can override single-name news.
Confidence: Medium
2. Celestica (CLS): a $40B 2027 outlook, but the gap is already shrinking
Pre-market: +15.5% to +17% in early trading (~$285–287), then +12.2% ($276.63) by 8:14 AM, vs. a $246.65 close. The stock fell ~5.1% in Monday's session before the report.
Thesis: This is the cleanest hard-data read on AI-rack demand before the hyperscalers report. Q3 revenue was $5.83B (+83%) and adjusted EPS $3.30 vs. ~$2.96–3.01 expected. The first 2027 outlook calls for $40.0B of revenue (+90%), $21.50 of adjusted EPS and a 9.0% adjusted operating margin, and management says customers' 2028 forecasts are "meaningfully higher." The revenue target beats the ~$37.98B consensus, but the EPS guide is below the ~$22.17 consensus (search summary). The gap has narrowed ~4–5 points since early trading as the Investor Day began at 8 AM. The open tests whether buyers defend it or the fade continues toward Monday's close.
Evidence: Celestica's Q3 release (SEC 8-K, summarized in the Oct. 26 close edition); TradingKey and Stocktwits (results, early moves); SA/TradingView and FinanceFeeds (search summaries: 2027 vs. consensus, the $246.65 close); StockMarketWatch (8:14 AM quote).
Risks: The 2026 free-cash-flow outlook was cut to $460M from $600M, and inventories rose $1.1B in Q3. A 9% margin on 90% growth implies heavy pass-through costs. Options priced a ±13.5% move (TipRanks, via the close edition), so much of it may already be in the price. Investor Day detail on margins or capex ($1.5–2.0B in 2027) could extend the fade.
Confidence: Medium
3. Meta (META): priced at $625 with Nvidia as anchor, so the offer price is the line to watch
Pre-market: −0.3% to −0.5% ($627.42–628.58) vs. a $630.56 close, which is ~0.4–0.6% above the $625 offer price (our arithmetic).
Thesis: Meta upsized the common stock to $25B (from $22.5B) at $625, a 0.9% discount to Monday's close (our arithmetic). It also sold $12.5B of 6.50% mandatory convertible preferred in $50 depositary shares, which convert around Aug. 15, 2029 at 1.3620–1.6000 shares per $1,000. That implies a $625 floor price and a ~$734 cap, a ~17.5% conversion premium (our arithmetic). Nvidia will invest $3B and expand the Muse partnership: Meta plans more Vera Rubin, Vera CPU, Spectrum-X and BlueField-4 deployments. A small discount and anchor demand suggest the deal cleared well. The question at the open is whether $625 acts as support as convertible-related hedging fades.
Evidence: Meta's pricing release (PR Newswire) and FWP on SEC EDGAR (search summaries); TipRanks (Nvidia and Muse terms; −0.43% pre-market); TradingKey (upsizing, $97.5B total); StockMarketWatch (most-active quote).
Risks: Forward sales plus a planned ~$60B Class C at-the-market program from Q1 2027 mean years of share supply. Nvidia funding a customer feeds the circular-financing debate. A break below $625 would leave new buyers under water. Microsoft and Alphabet capex commentary on Wednesday can reset the AI-spend narrative either way.
Confidence: Low–Medium
