1. How the Open Traded

The rally got bigger after the bell. Before the open, Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board director, effective immediately. The board said it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and is "confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk." Its independent investigation into Huang's ties to a Megaspeed executive "will proceed to conclusion," and it has set up a new National Security and Export Control Committee. That turned Sunday's political signal into an actual corporate decision, and futures jumped. Nasdaq 100 futures were about +7% just before 9:30, up from about +3.4% at 8:30.

Index levels. At the open, the S&P 500 was up about 3.9%, the Nasdaq about 5.8–7.3% and the Dow about 2.1%. The market kept rising into mid-morning. At about 10:20–11:00 AM ET, the S&P 500 was ~7,264–7,276 (+5.1 to +5.3%), the Nasdaq ~24,200–24,290 (+7.0 to +7.4%), the Dow ~49,630–49,690 (+3.1 to +3.2%) and the Russell 2000 ~2,661 (+3.1%). The VIX fell to about 27.3 (−24%). The 10-year yield was about 4.95% (+3.6 bp), gold about $3,672 (+4.5%), WTI about $80.30 (−3.7%) and Bitcoin about $63K (Yahoo Finance quote strip; CNN; Motley Fool).

Tone: broad, momentum-driven short covering, still the opposite of a fade. The S&P 500 is on pace to end a seven-day losing streak, and its gains so far match roughly two-thirds of Friday's 3.7% drop. Leadership is narrow at the top (NVDA alone was on pace to add about $850B of market value at the open, per Dow Jones via MarketScreener), but participation is wide: memory, foundry, equipment, neoclouds, AI power and the rest of the Mag 7 are all higher. Small caps are lagging tech. There are two things to watch for durability. First, the policy backdrop has not changed: a Fed hike on Dec. 9 is still priced at about 91–92%, and Wednesday's PCE print is the next macro test. Second, Reuters reports that lawmakers are criticizing the reinstatement, and Congress is already investigating the matter. The 9:45 flash PMI result was not found in the sources checked.

NVDA — NVIDIA  +34 to +36% (pre-mkt +7 to +9%)  Confidence: Medium

Thesis: This morning's pre-market brief treated the move as a "rhetorical pivot." It is now a real governance reversal: Huang is back, effective immediately, with the board citing input from the government. The gap more than quadrupled after the announcement and kept rising after the open, from about +30% to about +35%. A move that keeps extending into mid-morning on the largest stock in the market reads as continuation, not a fade, for now. Short covering is adding to it, after last week's shorting was the largest since April 2025 (Goldman). Even at about $169, the stock is still well below where it traded before the scandal; 24/7 Wall St. had it down about 39% over the past month before today's move.

Evidence: The Nvidia board statement (via Yahoo Finance). The stock opened about +30% and was at $168.73 (+34.4%) at 11:00 AM ET (Yahoo) and $170.35 (+35.7%) on the Motley Fool quote. Trump posted that the decision was "absolutely GREAT," and CNBC/MarketScreener report that he said he spoke directly to the board. The fundamental backdrop is strong: FQ3 revenue was $116B (+103%) with $130B guided for FQ4 (24/7 Wall St., citing the 8-K).

Key risks / what could flip it: (1) Reuters reports that lawmakers criticized the reinstatement, and a congressional probe that names Huang and Trump officials is already under way. Hearings or subpoenas could hit sentiment again. (2) The independent investigation is still open, and the new National Security and Export Control Committee suggests more compliance scrutiny, not less. (3) Export-licence decisions, the real economic issue, are unchanged. (4) History: 24/7 Wall St. notes Cisco's 24% one-day rally in 2000 came during a 79% decline. Gaps this large often give back part of the move within days. (5) The Fed hike risk is untouched.
