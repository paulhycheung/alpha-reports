1. How the Session Traded

The biggest relief rally since April 2025 held into the bell. The S&P 500 closed +4.73% at 7,237.17, ending a seven-session losing streak. The Nasdaq Composite closed +6.51% at 24,089.56, and the Dow gained about 1,450 points (+3.0%) (CNBC; CNN). All three had their best day since April 2025. AP described it as recovering roughly half of last week's plunge. The trigger was Nvidia's board reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO, effective immediately, 11 days after he resigned. The board said it had "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and is confident his return "poses no national security risk."

The shape of the day: a record gap higher at the open (QQQ opened almost 8% higher, its biggest opening jump since its 1999 launch), a high around 1:20 PM (S&P +5.1%, Nasdaq +7.0%), a slow fade to about 2:00 PM, and then stabilization into the close. The S&P closed slightly above its ~2:00 PM level (7,229), and Nvidia firmed from +30.6% at 1:57 PM to a +31.5% close. The afternoon fade did not turn into a late-day breakdown. The rally was concentrated in AI: Nvidia, Dell, Micron, Marvell and Nebius led. Defensive stocks lagged as money rotated back into tech: Chevron, UnitedHealth, J&J, Coca-Cola (about −1 to −2%) and Costco (about −1%) all fell (TipRanks).

Tone: strong, but a short-covering rally rather than a fundamental all-clear. Nomura's McElligott called it a "massive surge of MECHANICAL buying" that "guarantees us nothing." The Fed backdrop is unchanged. A 25 bp hike on Dec. 9 is still priced at about 91–92%, and the policy rate is 3.75–4.00%. NY Fed President Williams spoke today and, per a search summary, said inflation is still too high and the Fed has more work to do, leaving the door open to a December hike. Last week he ruled out a "dramatic" 50 bp move. After the bell, HPE beat and raised guidance but slipped about 2.4% after hours, an early sign that good news is already priced into the AI-server names.
