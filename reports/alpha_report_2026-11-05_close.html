1. Session Summary

The market fell into the close instead of stabilizing. The S&P 500 closed at 7,353.73 (−4.36%), its worst day since the April 2025 "Liberation Day" tariff shock (AP), and more than erased Wednesday's post-election rally, which was its best day in three months. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1,421 points to 24,315.00 (−5.52%), its largest one-day percentage drop since April 2025 (Kiplinger). The index is now 11.9% below its Oct. 6 record of 27,599.79, so the correction is confirmed. The Nasdaq-100 entered a correction on Monday. The Dow lost 1,833.06 points to 51,523.06 (−3.44%). Yahoo said the Dow was on track for its worst second session after a midterm in data going back to 1896. All 11 S&P sectors fell, with technology by far the worst (Kiplinger). The last 55 minutes made it worse: at ~3:05 PM the S&P was −4.04% and the Dow −3.28%, so another ~0.3 points was lost into the bell.

Cross-asset and tone: The PHLX Semiconductor Index was −10.65% at 3:47 PM (Yahoo). CNBC says the iShares Semiconductor ETF lost ~10.4%, its worst day since March 2020. The VIX was 26.95 (+62%) at 3:47 PM, its highest level since March (Kiplinger). The Russell 2000 was −4.65% at that time. Treasuries rallied: the 10-year fell to 4.83% from 4.90% (AP). Oil rose: Brent +3.9% to $98.29, and WTI was ~$90.7 (+2.9%). Gold was ~$4,111 (+0.9%) and bitcoin ~$76.1K (−5%). Retail investors capitulated: Vanda Research says individual investors sold a net $358M of US stocks by 2:58 PM, the most since March 17, 2020 ("total capitulation," Vanda's Viraj Patel). Some were still buying leveraged TQQQ/SOXL. Nomura's Charlie McElligott called the day "shooting the generals": hedge funds selling their 2026 momentum winners to lock in gains, with some trades "approaching the dreaded 'Round Trip' levels." Argent's Jed Ellerbroek told Reuters: "This feels like the capitulation phase for the data center capex beneficiary stocks… But I think the magnitude of the moves is outsized." Overseas: South Korea's Kospi fell 5.3% and Japan's Nikkei 1.8%. The Bank of England raised rates (8–1) for the first time since 2023.

1. Macro: S&P closes at the low (−4.36%, VIX ~27), and Friday's payrolls arrive at 8:30 AM

Thesis: The index closed near its worst levels of the day, so Friday opens with no relief rally already behind it, and the first scheduled catalyst is the October jobs report. Consensus is about +59K (Kiplinger) to +65K (the Reuters poll cited in earlier editions), with unemployment at 4.2%. Two things argue for big swings in both directions on Friday: today's signs of capitulation (record retail net selling since 2020, a +62% VIX jump, Nomura's "round trip" comment) and the lack of any bounce into the close.

Evidence: Kiplinger closing wrap and jobs preview (economist forecasts; ADP October +86K vs. 75K expected). AP closing update (worst day since April 2025; yields; Brent). Yahoo live blog (Vanda retail data; McElligott quotes; VIX 26.95 at 3:47 PM). CNBC closing levels (via search summaries).
