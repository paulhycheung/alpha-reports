1. SK hynix (SKHY), Micron (MU), SanDisk (SNDK): memory "sold out" through 2029, with a shortage seen into 2030
Pre-market: SKHY ADR +7.7% to +8% (≈$157 vs. a $145.36 close); MU +3% to +4% (≈$957–966 vs. ≈$929); SNDK ~+4%. In Asia, SK hynix rose ~10–11%, Samsung 8–9% and Kioxia ~14%.
Thesis: Q3 revenue was KRW 104.6T (+32% q/q, +328% y/y) and operating profit KRW 83.3T (+38% q/q, +632% y/y), an 80% margin. Both beat consensus (~98.3T and ~76.7T) by about 6% and 9% (our arithmetic). The bigger news is duration. Long-term agreements with 14 major customers now cover more than half of 2027–2028 capacity, priced within agreed ranges, and management expects demand to exceed supply through 2030; per Bloomberg, it said memory is sold out until 2029. That goes straight at the market's main worry, a price downturn once new capacity arrives around 2028 (TrendForce counts seven new SK hynix and Samsung plants, via CoinCentral). HBM4 should overtake HBM3E in shipments and revenue in Q4, and Q4 DRAM bit shipments are guided up by the mid-20s percent (CoinCentral). Returns are large: ~KRW 100T for 2026, a Q3 dividend of KRW 20,000 a share (half of it a special from Kioxia gains) and a new KRW 20T buyback (half to be cancelled, half for employee compensation). Last night's "beat-and-sell" in storage is already easing: Seagate went from −7% after hours to −2% to −4%.
Evidence: SK hynix's Q3 release (primary); Yahoo live blog; ZeroHedge (Bloomberg moves, Newsquawk consensus); CoinCentral (MU move, BofA's reiterated Buy and $1,550 target, call details); 24/7 Wall St.
Risks: Net income fell 58% q/q to KRW 39.8T, about 28% below the ~55.4T expected (Newsquawk via ZeroHedge). Q2's net income exceeded its operating profit, so it likely carried large non-operating gains that did not repeat (our inference). SKHY fell ~23–24% in the month before the print (prior edition), so short-covering may be part of the gap. SK hynix also plans to raise investment "significantly" next year, the supply response to watch. MU has more than tripled this year, and a hawkish Fed with the 10-year above 5% would hit long-duration semis first. Samsung and SanDisk report Thursday.
Confidence: Medium
2. Vertiv (VRT) and GE Vernova (GEV): AI power demand shows up in the order books
Pre-market: VRT +15% to +16% (≈$240–242 vs. a $208.56 close, itself +4.4% on Tuesday); GEV +2%.
Thesis: Vertiv's organic orders more than doubled. Q3 net sales rose 61% to $4.31B vs. a $3.76B FactSet consensus, about 15% above (our arithmetic). Diluted EPS rose 91% (adjusted +78%), and 2026 sales guidance was raised to $14.45–14.85B. CEO Giordano Albertazzi: "We are in the middle of the largest infrastructure buildout and it's accelerating." GE Vernova's revenue rose 27% to $12.6B (vs. ~$11.99B expected), organic orders rose 96% to $29B, and backlog reached $197B. It raised 2026 revenue guidance to $46.5–47.5B (from $45.5–46.5B) and free cash flow to $16–17B, lifted its buyback authorization to $20B, and agreed to buy high-voltage equipment maker Trench Group from Triton for $9.0B (closing expected in late 2027). Both are order-book evidence of AI-driven power and cooling demand, hours before Microsoft and Alphabet update their capex plans. JPMorgan's "Power" basket is among today's leaders.
Evidence: 24/7 Wall St. (Vertiv figures from its SEC exhibit and FactSet); Bloomberg via ZeroHedge (moves, Trench deal); GE Vernova's release and MarketScreener (search summaries).
Risks: A 16% gap on top of Tuesday's 4.4% gain invites first-hour profit-taking. Hyperscaler capex commentary tonight could swing the whole group either way. Supply chains are tight (ASM International called its own "stretched"), which limits how fast orders become revenue. GEV's modest +2% suggests much of its raise was expected.
Confidence: Medium for VRT holding most of its gap; Low–Medium for GEV
