1. Apple (AAPL): beat on sales, missed on margins. −3.7% to −4.5% (~$323–326)

Thesis: This is a cost story, not a demand story. Revenue ($114.5B, +12%), iPhone (+17%), Mac (+24%) and Services (a record ~$31.6B) all beat, and the December-quarter revenue guide (+7–10%) came in above the ~6.1% consensus. But the gross-margin guide of 45–46% (down from 47.9%) is below the ~46.3–47% the Street expected, and the CFO said rising memory costs explain most of the drop. CEO John Ternus called memory pricing "unprecedented." Tariff refunds also added ~1 point of margin and ~$0.05 of EPS to the quarter just reported, so underlying margins were softer than the headline. The gap is close to the ~4% move options implied, so the open will show whether investors treat this as a one-time reset or the start of an earnings-estimate cut cycle. Secondary angle: Apple closed Monday as the world's most valuable company, narrowly ahead of Nvidia (ZeroHedge). At these pre-market prices (AAPL ~−4%, NVDA ~+1.5%), Nvidia would likely reclaim the top spot, which tends to draw headlines and flows.

Evidence: MacRumors, 9to5Mac, Investing.com transcript and Yahoo (results, China $17.54B vs. $18.75B est., tariff-refund effect; search summary). TradingKey ($323.02, −4.52%, near a one-month low). Current Logic (−4.07%; puts at the 86th percentile of the past year; implied move just under 4% vs. a 2.5% two-year average). Analyst moves via CNBC and Investing.com (search summary): Goldman $380 from $390 (Buy), JPMorgan $355 from $360, Morgan Stanley $350 from $355 (OW), BofA $370 and Evercore $380 kept; Aletheia Sell at $250.

Risks: Most analysts kept Buy ratings, and the revenue guide is strong, so dip buyers may step in once the open passes. Memory costs are an industry-wide input; if peers (or AMD tonight) confirm that memory pricing is easing, the margin worry fades. The ~$323 area is near a one-month low, where earlier pullbacks have stalled.
