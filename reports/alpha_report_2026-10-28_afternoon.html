1. How the Afternoon Has Traded

The Fed delivered a hawkish hold, and stocks dipped, rallied while Warsh spoke, then settled near where they started. Oracle's Investor Day was the biggest afternoon surprise. The FOMC held the federal funds rate at 3.75%–4.00% in a 10–2 vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a 25 bp hike. The September vote had been unanimous. The statement changed in three ways. It upgraded growth to a "strong" pace from "solid." It dropped "today's policy action will support" a return to 2%. In its place, the Committee now says "the accumulation of more information will help" it set the right stance for a "timely" (previously "timelier") return to target (Yahoo's statement comparison). At the press conference, Warsh said Q3 growth is "likely running above 3%." He said inflation "has been running above the committee's objective for close to six years" and that "it's our responsibility to get inflation down." He also said energy prices "have moved higher again" and that the Fed's job is to keep them from broadening across the economy (TNND/WKRC). He said politics played "no role" ahead of next Tuesday's midterms. He also announced that from 2027 some FOMC meetings will be strategy-only, with no rate vote: one in 2027 and two in 2028 (Yahoo).

1. Oracle (ORCL): Investor Day turns a flat tape into a double-digit gain
Move: About flat until the 2:21 PM release ($130.79, +0.20% at 12:10). After it: +8.8% to $142.01 at 2:31 (Pluang), "more than 8%" (Reuters, 2:48), a +9.6% peak (Bloomberg via PANews), then +11.79% to $145.92 on 53.81M shares (Investing.com). Each print was higher than the last.
Thesis: The new information is the funding path, not the revenue target. The FY30 targets ($225B+ revenue, 40%+ non-GAAP operating margin, $21+ non-GAAP EPS) match the headline numbers from last October's analyst day. What changed today: Oracle expects positive free cash flow in 2H FY28 and $30B+ in FY29, with growth self-funded from FY29. Capex peaks in FY27–FY28. Additional capital-market funding through FY30 is capped at $20B ($10B equity, $10B gross debt, excluding refinancing), on top of the $20B at-the-market raise already done. Net debt is targeted below 3.5x adjusted EBITDA in FY29 (company release). Bloomberg reports that investors had expected FY29 free cash flow to stay negative, so the cash-flow path is an outright surprise. That matters because Oracle had become a focal point of AI-financing worries, with heavy reliance on OpenAI (Reuters). The stock came in down about a third this year (Reuters) and ~60% below last year's Investor Day close (Motley Fool). Valuation context (our arithmetic): at $145.92 the stock is ~18x reaffirmed FY27 non-GAAP EPS of $8.10 and ~7x the FY30 target of $21+. CoreWeave's +5.2% may be a read-across on AI-cloud funding risk (our inference).
Evidence: Oracle Investor Day release (via StockTitan/PR Newswire); Reuters via Investing.com; Bloomberg via PANews; Pluang; Investing.com most-active and gainers lists.
Risks / what could reverse it into the close: Every target is forward-looking, and the dilution is real: $20B already raised plus $10B more equity planned. The net debt definition includes vendor financing and leases, and financing from vendors and customers shifts risk rather than removing it. Last year's analyst day raised targets and the stock fell more than 7% the next day. A gain that kept building into the afternoon invites profit-taking at the close. Tonight's Microsoft and Alphabet capex commentary could reset sentiment on AI infrastructure either way.
Confidence: Medium that it holds most of the gain into the close; Low for follow-through beyond tonight's hyperscaler reports
