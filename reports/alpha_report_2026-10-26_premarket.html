1. Meta Platforms (META): a $95 billion equity plan, and the stock is rising
Pre-market: fell as much as ~4% after the announcement, recovered to about +1% by 7:43 AM ET (The Next Web) and was +2.4% by ~8:15 AM (Bloomberg via ZeroHedge), vs. a $642.33 close. Meta fell about 9% last week, its worst week in four years.
Thesis: Last week's selloff was about how Meta would pay for $135–145B of 2026 capex after free cash flow turned negative in Q3. Its 5-year CDS has roughly doubled since August to about 120 bp. This morning Meta sized the answer. $35B launches now: $22.5B of Class A common via forward sale agreements, so cash arrives later (settlement by Nov. 19, 2027 at the latest), and $12.5B of mandatory convertible preferred converting around Aug. 15, 2029. A planned ~$60B at-the-market program of non-voting Class C shares is meant to start in Q1 2027 and is not yet finalized. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom indicated non-binding interest in up to $5B. The move from −4% to green suggests investors prefer a known equity overhang to open-ended debt. The test at the open is whether the stock holds green through pricing.
Evidence: Meta's launch press release (PR Newswire, linked by The Next Web) and SEC FWP/424B5 filings (search results); The Next Web (greenshoe of up to $3.375B of common; $97B of operating cash flow in the first nine months; ~$82B of cash and securities); Bloomberg via ZeroHedge (CDS, the market reaction, Goldman's TMT desk on multi-year equity needs).
Risks: Large offerings usually price at a discount, and buyers of mandatory convertibles often hedge by shorting common, which can cap the stock into pricing (pricing time not confirmed). The ~$60B at-the-market program is a multi-year supply overhang. The chipmakers' interest is non-binding and circular, since Meta is their customer. Microsoft and Alphabet capex commentary Wednesday can reset the AI-funding narrative in either direction.
Confidence: Medium
2. Brazil reprices after Lula's win (EWZ; XP, STNE, PAGS, NU, ITUB, BBD, PBR, MELI)
Pre-market: EWZ ≈ −27%; XP −46% to −51%; STNE −34% to −39%; PAGS −29% to −34%; BBD ≈ −28%; ITUB −25% to −28%; NU −23% to −25%; PBR ≈ −22%; MELI −11% to −14%; VALE only ≈ −3.6%.
Thesis: Lula came from behind to win 51.8%–48.2%, catching consensus leaning the other way (JPMorgan had said it favored Brazil). Banks, fintechs and state-linked names are being repriced for fiscal risk and higher-for-longer local rates, while exporters like Vale are barely down. Our arithmetic: Goldman's "extreme" scenario (Brazilian equities −15% in local terms and USD/BRL +10%) implies about −23% in dollar terms. EWZ indicated at −27% is already beyond that. Brazil's B3 cash session typically opens at 10:00 local time, about 9:00 AM ET while the US is on daylight time, so local price discovery should set the tone before the US open. The open is a capitulation-or-continuation test.
Evidence: Bloomberg via ZeroHedge (EWZ −27%; B3 widened trading-range limits; Bolsonaro is calling for the vote to be annulled; ING's Chris Turner sees USD/BRL gapping to 5.20–5.25 and "double-digit losses" for the Bovespa). Risks: Pre-market ADR prints are thin and can overshoot either way; XP's −46% to −51% spread shows the dispersion. A quick concession, a market-friendly finance-minister pick or central-bank action could spark a violent relief bounce, while a contested result could deepen the selloff. Contagion is already visible: Santander −4% in Madrid, and MELI and NU are widely held US growth names.
Confidence: Medium for volatility; Low for direction
3. AI funding split: chips and memory bid, neoclouds sold, with Anthropic's S-1 due ~9 AM (NVDA, AVGO, AMD, MU, SKHY vs. CRWV, NBIS, IREN)
Pre-market: NVDA +0.4% to +1.4%, AVGO +1.5%, AMD +1.3%; MU +1.4%, SKHY +2.9%, SNDK +1.3% (~6 AM); CRWV −3.6% to −5.5%, NBIS −3.1% to −4%, IREN −2.8%.
Thesis: The market is separating AI spenders that can fund themselves from levered builders. Musk said SpaceX closed $40B of GPU debt financing because of its investment-grade profile, but warned that debt markets have "almost entirely shut off access to money" for companies without substantial revenue outside AI data centers and that "200+ neoclouds" face consolidation. It reported Q3 revenue of $16.8B, up 45% quarter over quarter (Reuters, via search summary), and its disclosures on compute commitments are read-throughs for NVDA, AVGO and AMD and for its investors and cloud partners AMZN and GOOGL. Memory has its own support: BofA reiterated MU Buy ($1,550 target; long-term agreements now cover ~35% of sales), and SK hynix may pay a ~$12B special dividend (not yet decided; reports Wednesday).
Evidence: CNBC live blog (search summary: NVDA, AVGO, AMD moves tied to the Anthropic filing); TipRanks (memory, neoclouds, Anthropic timing per Bloomberg); Investing.com and Bloomberg via ZeroHedge (Musk quotes; CoreWeave bonds briefly yielded above 16% on Oct. 15, per TipRanks).
Risks: Anthropic's filing already slipped once, and its disclosed losses (the WSJ reported a net loss of more than $80B in H1 2026) could revive AI-ROI worries instead of easing them. Goldman reports record two-week net selling in global tech and possible CTA supply. CCC yields above 16% show credit stress is spreading. NVDA closed near its lows Friday on ~0.55× volume, and early strength has faded before.
Confidence: Low–Medium
4. Apple (AAPL): Jefferies bear note one week before earnings
Pre-market: −1.2% to −1.7% (≈ $343.44 vs. a $347.73 close).
Thesis: Jefferies' Edison Lee (Underperform, $270.62 target) expects the September quarter to beat: revenue +15% vs. Apple's ~11% guidance, helped by China iPhone sales +15% y/y as buyers moved ahead of September price increases. He sees the December quarter at only +2% revenue and −4% EPS, below the Street. His reasons are weaker iPhone 18 Pro/Pro Max demand (lower supply-chain orders, shorter delivery times), soft early signals for the DUO foldable (Hong Kong resale premiums of 10–17% vs. 30–50% expected) and memory costs (product gross margin −4 points in FY27). That moves the Nov. 2 debate from the print to the guidance.
Evidence: TipRanks summary of the Jefferies note (Oct. 26). Background from earlier in October: Nikkei Asia reported October component orders cut ~15%, and Morgan Stanley trimmed its target to $355 (TipRanks, Oct. 9). The average analyst target (~$337) sits below Friday's close.
Risks: The Underperform rating is not new, and Lee himself expects a beat. Buybacks and defensive flows have supported AAPL, which briefly topped NVDA in market value on Friday. Megacap flows today may be dominated by the Meta and Anthropic headlines.
Confidence: Low–Medium
