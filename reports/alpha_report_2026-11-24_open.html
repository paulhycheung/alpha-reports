1. How the First Hour Traded

A weak open that quickly turned into a rotation rather than a broad sell-off. At the open, the Dow fell about 0.6%, the S&P 500 1.3% and the Nasdaq 1.8% (Yahoo). Within 30–45 minutes the broad market recovered about half or more of that: by about 10:17 AM the Dow was +0.37%, the Russell 2000 +0.42% and the S&P 500 only −0.6%, while the Nasdaq was still −1.55%. QQQ improved from −2.07% at 9:01 AM to −1.40% at 10:01 AM, and DIA was +0.53% (StockTitan). The VIX eased to about 27.4 from ~30 in pre-market. The 10-year yield was flat at about 4.92%.

The selling was concentrated in AI hardware, and it got worse after the open. AMD (−8.0%), Micron (−7.7%), Nvidia (−5.6% at 10:01, −6.7% by ~10:17), HPE (−13 to −14%), Dell (−10.8%) and Analog Devices (−15.6%) all traded below their pre-market levels. Retailers that beat went the other way: Abercrombie +22%, Dick's +14%, Kohl's +13%, Dollar Tree +10%, Burlington +9%. The 10:00 AM Conference Board consumer confidence index came in at 103.3 against 92.1 expected, up 11.4 points to its highest since January 2025, which supports the consumer side of the rotation. Fed-funds futures still price about a 95% chance of a 25 bp hike on Dec. 9 (Yahoo). UBS's Mark Haefele summed up the theme: expect "greater differentiation" between profitable AI platforms and companies that depend on debt or "continued speculative growth" (via StockTitan, citing CNBC).
