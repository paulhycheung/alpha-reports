1. How the Session Has Traded

The rally has held, but it stopped growing after the first hour. The QQQ opened almost 8% higher, its biggest opening jump since it launched in 1999, and the SPY opened about 5% higher, the second-biggest Monday open since 1993 after Oct. 13, 2008 (Yahoo Finance / Jared Blikre). Since then the indexes have moved sideways in a narrow band. At 11:03 AM both funds were slightly below their opening prices (StockTitan). The S&P 500 was +5.00% at 11:31 (Motley Fool), +4.70% near noon (The Fly) and +4.97% at 7,254 at 12:33 PM (Yahoo). The Nasdaq was +6.4% to +6.9% over the same stretch, the Dow ~+3.1 to +3.2% (49,684), and the Russell 2000 +3.1% (2,662.5). Small caps are still lagging tech by about 4 points.

Cross-asset moves are consistent with a relief rally. At 12:33 PM: VIX 27.33 (−23.9%), gold $3,663 (+4.2%), WTI $80.82 (−3.1%), Bitcoin ~$61.7K (+1.6%), and the 10-year yield at 4.944% (+3.2 bp) (Yahoo). The Motley Fool's midday note says the 10-year "declined 12 basis points to 4.92%," but that appears to repeat Friday's move, so this brief uses the Yahoo reading.

New macro data was soft. The 9:45 S&P Global flash PMIs for November came in at 54.6 composite (55.9 prior), the slowest expansion since July. Services were 54.7, below the 55.6 forecast (56.1 prior), and manufacturing was 54.5, in line with forecasts. S&P Global said input costs eased, helped by lower fuel prices, but warned that price measures still point to inflation above 2% (Newsquawk; QNC News). The data does not change the Fed picture: a Dec. 9 hike is still priced at about 91–92%.

Tone: a strong but "mechanical" rally. Nomura's Charlie McElligott calls the move "a massive surge of MECHANICAL buying" driven by short covering and dealers unwinding hedges. He warned that the bounce "guarantees us nothing" while inflation, high borrowing costs and cooling AI-capex expectations remain (Yahoo). Yardeni also cut his S&P 500 target after last week's selloff (TipRanks headline). Still to come this afternoon: the $69B 2-year note auction (1:00 PM), NY Fed President Williams (4:00 PM, the last scheduled Fed speaker before the December blackout), and after-the-close results from HPE, Zoom and Agilent.
