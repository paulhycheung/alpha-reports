1. How the Open Traded

Payrolls: October nonfarm payrolls came in at +165K, far above the ~59K–65K consensus. September was revised up to +60K (from +29K), and August and September combined were revised up by 84K. Unemployment fell to 4.1% (4.2% expected), and wages rose 0.4% m/m (0.3% expected). A strong labor market keeps a December Fed hike on the table. The 10-year yield rose to ~4.86–4.88%.

At the bell: The Dow opened −0.94% (51,036.69), the S&P 500 +0.26% (7,373.17) and the Nasdaq Composite +1.23% (24,614.53). The iShares semiconductor ETF (SOXX) was up +4.9% by 9:40 AM. By ~11:00 AM the indices had firmed: S&P 500 ~7,410–7,415 (+0.8%), Nasdaq ~24,660–24,690 (+1.4% to +1.5%), and the Dow back to about flat (~51,520–51,545). The Russell 2000 was up about 0.3–0.5%, and the VIX was down about 12–15% to ~24. Brent was ~$97, WTI ~$90, gold ~$4,064 (−1.2%) and bitcoin ~$75.6K.

Tone: The rally broadened, but chips lost some of their opening gains. Semis had their biggest gains in the first 10 minutes, then gave back part of them; memory faded most. Meanwhile, the platform mega-caps that were red pre-market (AAPL, MSFT/AMZN about flat) moved higher, and the Dow recovered almost all of its opening loss. The market took the hot jobs report calmly: yields rose, but stocks barely reacted (Investing.com). This looks less like a one-sector reflex bounce and more like broad short-covering and a drop in volatility. TheStreet's Rev Shark ("Don't Confuse a Bounce With a Bottom") and the ~$50B of vol-control selling cited pre-market are reasons to doubt it will last.
