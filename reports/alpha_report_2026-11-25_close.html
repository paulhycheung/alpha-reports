1. Session Summary

A violent intraday round trip that ended close to flat, with rotation underneath. Early in the session the Nasdaq was down more than 4%, the Dow down more than 1,000 points (ABC), and the S&P 500 touched 6,970. The triggers were a hot core PCE (3.3% vs. 3.2% expected), the USTR's proposed new tariffs on 14 economies, and continued selling of AI names. Prices recovered steadily from late morning. By the close the S&P 500 was roughly flat, the Dow finished above 50,000, and the Russell 2000 gained 0.65%. The Nasdaq still lost about 0.7%. 24/7 Wall St. describes it as money "rotating out of tech into old-economy names." The Dow and the Nasdaq were more than a percentage point apart.

The split was clear by industry. AI suppliers and infrastructure lost: chip equipment (LRCX −6.5%, AMAT −4.4%), memory (SNDK −5% to −6%, MU −4% to −4.6%), ORCL (−5%), NVDA (−3.8% to −4%), and the hyperscalers AMZN (−3.1%), GOOGL (−2.0%) and MSFT (−1.25%). Old-economy names, hardware integrators, META and speculative small caps gained: IBM, Salesforce and Sherwin-Williams led the Dow (CNBC via StockTitan), META rose about 3%, DELL +0.6%, HPE +1.75%, and JOBY, DKNG, AMC and AXON each rose 7–12%. In the final hour the Nasdaq improved slightly from −0.88% at 3:08 PM (Yahoo), and NVDA improved from −4.9% to about −3.8%. The 10-year was ~4.85% and the VIX ~27.8 at 3:08 PM. US markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving. Stocks reopen Friday for a session that ends at 1:00 PM ET.
