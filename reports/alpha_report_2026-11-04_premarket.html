1. Overnight & Global Summary

Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate, and futures went from a sharp sell-off to a rally. On the first results, Nasdaq-100 futures fell as much as 2.4% and S&P 500 futures as much as 1.4% (Bloomberg/Reuters via search summaries). By morning both had reversed. Yahoo's quote strip (~7:41 AM) showed S&P futures at 7,670 (+0.96%), Dow futures +1.70%, Russell 2000 futures +1.48% and Nasdaq futures only +0.44%. Investing.com's cash CFDs (~8:40 AM) read about the same: US 30 +1.87%, US 500 +0.97%, US Tech 100 +0.36%. The rebound is led by the Dow and small caps, with tech lagging. Analysts cite three reasons for the recovery: President Trump's conciliatory call (paired with a warning he'd "just veto everything"), the expectation of gridlock, and the fact that the new Congress isn't seated until January (TheStreet, Capital.com). The VIX fell to ~18.3–18.5 (−11–12%). The 10-year yield eased to ~4.92% (about −3 bp from Tuesday's 4.958%; TipRanks, Investing.com). The dollar index fell 0.6% to ~101.1. Gold rose ~3% to ~$4,050–4,060 and silver +4.3% (Investing.com, Yahoo). WTI fell to ~$94.5 (−1.5–1.7%) on signs of US–Iran diplomacy, even though the IRGC reported four tankers on fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz (TheStreet). Bitcoin was ~$78.2K.

Asia: Japan bore the brunt. The Nikkei 225 was down as much as 3.6–4.8% intraday, its lowest since July 29, then recovered to close −1.42% at 62,188.63 (Topix −0.89%). AI supply-chain names led the decline: Advantest fell ~6–8%, Tokyo Electron ~5–6% and SoftBank ~7–9% intraday (Reuters via The Star; Capacity). China rallied: the Shanghai Composite rose +2.16%, Shenzhen +3.15% and the CSI 300 +2.31%. The Hang Seng gained +1.67%, the KOSPI +0.62% and India's Nifty 50 +0.63% (TipRanks, Fortune). Europe: the Stoxx 600 rose +1.05–1.14% and the FTSE 100 +0.76–0.85% in early trade, led by renewables (Fortune, TipRanks). Tuesday's US close for reference: S&P 500 7,568.92 (+0.36%), Nasdaq +0.83%, Dow 51,786.72 (−0.38%). Tone: a relief rally with a clear rotation. Industrials, small caps, renewables, health care and precious metals are bid. AI-infrastructure builders (neoclouds, data-center developers) and the Musk-linked stocks are being sold on policy risk. AMD's earnings, on the other hand, are holding up chip designers.

Research observations only: names worth watching at the 9:30 AM open. They are not recommendations to buy or sell.

1. AMD: the beat and raise holds up through an AI-policy scare. +5.7% to +7.3%

Thesis: AMD is the clearest idiosyncratic catalyst in the fixed watchlist. Q3 revenue was a record $13.4B (+45% y/y) vs. ~$13.1B expected. Adj. EPS was $2.09 vs. $1.95. Data Center revenue rose 97% to ~$8.5B vs. ~$8.2B expected. The Q4 guide of ~$17B ±3% is above the ~$16.2–16.3B consensus even at the low end. On the call, Lisa Su put 2027 revenue growth above 80%, ahead of the 77% LSEG consensus (Reuters via Investing.com). What makes it notable is that it is holding a ~6–7% gain on a morning when Democratic control of Congress and a Texas governor pledging a data-center moratorium hit almost every other AI-infrastructure name. The market appears to be separating "who sells the chips" from "who builds the buildings." The key test at the open is whether AMD holds above roughly $500–505 (≈+3.5–4.5%), or whether it fades again as it did after its last two reports.

Evidence: AMD Q3 release (prior edition). Investing.com pre-market movers (+7.3%, 2027 >80% vs. 77% LSEG, ~$58B of added market value if held). TheStreet live blog (+5.73% at 7:36 AM). TipRanks (+6.2%; analyst moves listed include JPMorgan PT raised to $575 from $550 and UBS PT trimmed to $700 from $730). MoneyCheck (~$518). Yahoo live blog (+6%).

Risks: AMD has reversed lower after beating on each of its last two reports. The Q4 gross-margin guide steps down to ~55% from 56%. Gaming fell 40%, and memory costs are pressuring PCs. One Investing.com summary already described the stock slipping from its >7% peak. The stock is up ~126% YTD, which leaves room for "sell the news." If the 10 AM ISM prices component runs hot, long-duration semis could lose their bid.

Confidence: Medium (stays green at the open). Low (holds the full gap through the close)

2. Tesla (TSLA): a political-risk discount on the Musk-linked stocks. −4.4% to −5.9%; SpaceX (SPCX) −4.7% to −7%

Thesis: Tesla is the largest decliner in the fixed watchlist, and the reason is political, not fundamental. Elon Musk spent a record ~$474M on the 2026 midterms (TheStreet) and more than $250M with Republican-aligned groups in Q3 alone (TipRanks). Democratic control of both chambers creates a platform for investigations of Trump's allies, and Yahoo named Musk specifically. It also adds friction to the proposed Tesla–SpaceX combination; one analyst told the WSJ Congress "could slow it down via extra scrutiny." Both companies are based in Texas, which just elected a Democratic governor. The question at the open is whether this is a one-day de-risking that buyers step into (Congress isn't seated until January, and Trump retains the veto) or the start of a longer derating of the "Musk premium." An Investing.com headline says Morgan Stanley turned bullish on SpaceX this morning (details not verified), which could limit the downside on the Musk-linked group.

Evidence: TheStreet live blog (TSLA $378.93, −4.91%; SPCX $133.92, −5.23% at 7:09 AM). Yahoo/Fast Company/CoinCentral via search (−4.4% to −5.5%). CNBC pre-market headline via search (Tesla nearly −6%, SpaceX more than −7%). TipRanks (−4.4% / −4.7%). Yahoo live blog (Democratic agenda targeting Trump's allies).

Risks: Gaps driven by political headlines often partially fill once cash trading starts, especially with the S&P futures up ~1%. Tesla is heavily owned by retail investors and tends to move further than expected in both directions. Nothing in Tesla's operating outlook changed overnight.

Confidence: Medium (underperforms the S&P today). Low (direction from the open)

3. The AI build-out split: data-center developers sold, chip designers bought. CRWV −3 to −7%, IREN ~−6%, ORCL ~−1.2% vs. NVDA +0.4–1.2%, AVGO +1%

Thesis: The election's main market effect is a new political risk premium on the physical AI build-out. Texas, which accounts for more than 25% of planned US data-center capacity by 2030 (Yahoo), elected a governor who pledged a moratorium on new data-center construction. Before the vote, Reuters counted 41 local ballot measures in 10 states aimed at curbing data centers, and Ballotpedia noted that every such measure decided earlier this year went against development. Results for those measures were not yet compiled. Investors are also weighing tighter AI regulation, changes to tech tax incentives and antitrust scrutiny (Investing.com). That hits the companies that need permits and power: neoclouds, data-center REITs and developers, and Oracle, whose $20B stock-and-debt raise settles today. Japan's AI supply chain (Advantest, Tokyo Electron, SoftBank) took a 5–9% hit intraday. Chip designers with fresh demand evidence (AMD, Super Micro and Arista overnight) are holding up. Watch whether the spread between developers and chip designers widens after the open or closes as investors decide policy changes are slow and mostly local.

Evidence: Investing.com pre-market movers (CRWV, APLD, NBIS, ORCL figures; regulatory concerns). CNBC pre-market headline via search (CRWV −7%, IREN −6%, NBIS ~−5%). TipRanks (NVDA +1.2%, AVGO +1.0%; Hinojosa moratorium pledge). Yahoo live blog (Texas >25% of planned capacity). Reuters via bdnews24 and Axios (41 ballot measures). Capacity and The Star (Nikkei AI names).

Risks: Neoclouds closed +7% on Tuesday (CRWV, NBIS), so part of today's drop just retraces that gain. Federal policy will take months to change, and the gridlock argument cuts both ways. Nvidia was down more than 1% earlier this morning, so the chip-designer bid is not secure if Nasdaq futures slip again.

Confidence: Medium (the theme shapes today's AI tape). Low (persistence beyond this week)

4. Hard numbers vs. political fear: Super Micro (SMCI) +7–8% and Arista (ANET) +6–10% after hours

Thesis: The two overnight AI-hardware reports were both large beats with strong guides. Super Micro reported Sept.-quarter revenue of $19.9B (+298% y/y), above both the $16.26B consensus and its own $18.5–19.5B preannouncement. Adjusted gross margin rose to 12.8% from 9.5%. The next-quarter guide ($19.5–21.5B) and the full-year guide ($72–82B, up from the $65–72B it gave in July) are both well above consensus. Arista grew revenue 58.5%, its fastest since the IPO, guided Q4 to $3.9–4.0B vs. $3.6B, raised FY26 for the fourth time to $13.3B, and gave an initial FY27 outlook of ~39% growth. Melius raised its PT to $305 and Morgan Stanley to $230 (the latter citing an expanded TAM of more than $220B). The question for the open is whether order books like these outweigh the policy narrative in candidate 3. If SMCI and ANET hold their gaps while CRWV and IREN stay weak, it confirms the split described there.

Evidence: Investing.com pre-market movers (SMCI figures, +7%). TipRanks headline ("nearly 8%"). Investing.com and Benzinga via search (ANET results, +6% / +10.2% to $187 after hours; Melius and Morgan Stanley PTs).

Risks: SMCI's 12.8% gross margin is thin, the stock has a history of disclosure and governance problems, and it is volatile. Arista's after-hours range (+6% to +10%) suggests the reaction hasn't settled. Both depend on hyperscaler and neocloud capex, which is exactly what the election may slow.

Confidence: Medium (both open higher). Low (both hold their full gaps)
