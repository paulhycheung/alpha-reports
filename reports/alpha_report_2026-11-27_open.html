4. Alpha Candidates

Framed as "worth investigating further," not as recommendations. Only about two hours of trading remain after this snapshot, and early-close sessions can see erratic moves into 1:00 PM.

NVDA — Nvidia −1% to −2% (pre-market +2% to +4.7%) Confidence: Medium

Thesis: fade, with relative weakness likely to persist into the close. Nvidia is the only major AI name to fully reverse its gap while memory and platform names held. That points to stock-specific selling, not a market-wide failure of the bounce. The market appears to be pricing a new, concrete revenue risk on top of the governance story.

Evidence: The New York Times and FT, via Semafor and Yahoo, report that Nvidia froze server orders for buyers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. Dell and Supermicro told customers the orders would not ship. Semafor notes Nvidia's Singapore arm booked $104B in 2025. Reuters says it is unclear whether the halt is temporary while compliance work is done. A Bloomberg report (pre-market brief) separately found Blackwell racks at a Shanghai site linked to a blacklisted firm. Economic Times reports: "Nvidia shares failed to sustain their early recovery… rose more than 2% in premarket trading." A search summary says S&P Global put Nvidia's AA rating on CreditWatch negative; this was not verified against an S&P release.

What could flip this read: A clarification from Nvidia or Commerce that the halt is a short licensing pause, or a sign that the stock has stabilised and short sellers are covering into a thin close, could produce a sharp late reclaim. The stock has moved +31% and −10% within a single week, so neither direction is reliable.

SNDK / MU (with WDC, SKHY) — Memory SNDK +10% to +14%, MU +4% to +6% Confidence: Medium

Thesis: has legs into midday. Memory was this morning's top pick for "most oversold, highest beta," and it is the one group where the pre-market gap held and extended after the open. SanDisk is still down about 48% over a month and Micron about 30%, which leaves plenty of room for short-covering.

Evidence: 24/7 Wall St. quotes SNDK at $80.99 (+10%), with a later widget showing $84.00 (+14.1%). MU is quoted at $633–635 (+5–6%), WDC +5% and SKHY +5%. Micron's CFO said in September that more than 75% of 2027 shipments are committed and that he expects the market to stay tight through 2028.

Risks: TrendForce published a report today (Nov 27) saying DRAM contract price increases could narrow through 2027 and prices could decline in 2028. BNP Paribas downgraded MU on Nov 24. XTB's Kathleen Brooks calls the recovery "technical." STX is down about 2%, so storage is not moving uniformly. Holiday liquidity could amplify a late reversal.
