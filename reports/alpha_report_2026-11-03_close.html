1. AMD: a beat and raise on revenue, offset by a one-point gross-margin step-down. +3.6–3.7% into the print; after-hours direction unverified

Thesis: AMD's report is the most important development for tomorrow's semiconductor tape. Q3 revenue was a record $13.4B (+45% y/y, +16% q/q), above the top of its own $12.7–13.3B guide and the ~$13.08–13.09B consensus. Non-GAAP EPS was $2.09 (+74% y/y) vs. ~$1.95–1.97. Data Center revenue nearly doubled (+97% to $8.5B), and that segment's operating income was $2.88B (~34% margin) vs. $1.07B a year ago. The key number is the guide: Q4 revenue of ~$17B ±3%, implying ~66% y/y growth and ~27% sequential growth. Even the low end (~$16.5B) is above the ~$16.2–16.3B consensus cited in previews. The weak spot is margin: Q4 non-GAAP gross margin is guided to ~55%, down from 56%. On a day when Apple and Zebra both blamed memory costs for margin pressure, that step-down will be read as either MI450/Helios rack mix or component inflation. The call commentary should show which. AMD also announced agreements to acquire World Labs (model research) and Taalas (inference silicon), and its release lists strategic collaborations with Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, HPE and Kodiak AI. Lisa Su said she expects growth "to accelerate further in the fourth quarter as we scale Helios and 'Venice' production."
