2. AMD & TSM: export-control risk is now driving chip stocks one by one

Move: AMD ~−9% at the close. TSMC was about −5% in Taipei since Monday (ADR close not verified).

Thesis: Two separate China-related headlines hit chips today. Hunterbrook disclosed a short in AMD over whether its chips reach a blacklisted Chinese server maker through intermediaries. Separately, the FT reported that TSMC and Samsung paused some shipments to Chinese customers while they seek licenses under a new BIS rule. The rule moves the "headquarters test" onto foundries, so designers headquartered in China can no longer clear advanced logic chips through approved packaging firms. China was 5% of TSMC's Q3 revenue (about $2.3B), down from 8% a year earlier. The APEC talks ended without progress on semiconductors (Yahoo). Together, these make AMD's drop look more like a pricing of policy risk than a one-off short report. Retail selling to fund ANT purchases (Vanda) may have added to the size of the move.

Evidence: Yahoo live blog (AMD −9%; Hunterbrook; APEC stalemate; Vanda flow). Newsquawk headline (FT: TSMC/Samsung pause shipments). BeInCrypto via Yahoo (TSMC −5% since Monday; 5% China revenue share; BIS rule mechanics).

Risks / what flips it: A quick BIS license approval for TSMC customers, or an AMD rebuttal backed by documents, could reverse part of the move. Hunterbrook itself said its findings "don't establish an export-law violation" (per the Midday edition). Flow-driven selling usually stops once the buying it funds is done.

Confidence: Medium
