1. How the Session Has Traded

Chips kept falling through the morning, but the rest of the market recovered after 11 AM. The open edition called this a rotation rather than a broad sell-off, and the second half of the morning supports that read. Chips and the Nasdaq-100 set new lows at 10:22–10:26 AM and again at 11:03 AM, when the Nasdaq-100 was down about 3.2% and the S&P 500 1.3%. At that low the Dow had given back almost all of its gain. After 11:34 the indexes rallied: by 12:09 the S&P 500 had cut its loss to 0.64%, and by 12:44 PM the S&P 500 was −0.31%, the Nasdaq Composite −1.29%, the Dow +0.90% and the Russell 2000 (IWM) +0.76% (Sell The News, timeline drawn from CNBC/WSJ/Bloomberg). Amgen, Nike and Salesforce supported the Dow.

Breadth was positive even with the index down. Bloomberg reports the equal-weight S&P 500 was up as much as 0.6%, with more than 300 S&P 500 stocks higher and fewer than 200 lower. Health care and consumer staples led the sectors, and technology was the worst, down about 4%. The pain is concentrated: the semiconductor ETF SMH was still −6.5% at 12:44 even after bouncing off its low. Bloomberg puts the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 9% and 40% below its June peak (TradingNews had −6.2% near noon; the difference is probably timing). The Nasdaq-100 is about 16% below its record, close to bear-market territory, and Bloomberg says the S&P 500 is heading for its worst November since 2008.

A new headline: tariffs. Bloomberg, Reuters, the FT and the WSJ report that the administration may announce, as soon as today or Wednesday, new Section 301 tariffs on China, the EU and about 14 other economies. They would replace the levies the Supreme Court struck down. Bloomberg's sources say the rates will be targeted by country and will not go above levels set in existing trade deals. Japan, Korea and the EU could face an extra 2.5–5%. That cap is probably part of why the market bounced instead of selling off further.

Cross-asset moves (Yahoo header, ~12:03 PM): the 10-year yield was 4.89% (−2.6 bp), the VIX 28.0 (+5.4%, down from about 30 pre-market), gold $3,566 (−2.3%), Bitcoin ~$59.2K (−3.9%) and WTI $80.75 (+0.7%). Fed-funds futures still price about a 95% chance of a 25 bp hike on Dec. 9. Still to come: the $70B 5-year note auction at 1:00 PM (the result was not available when this was written), Dell, HP Inc., Autodesk, Workday and Nutanix after the close, and October PCE and Q3 GDP on Wednesday at 8:30 AM.
