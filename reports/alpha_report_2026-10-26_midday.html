2. JetBlue (JBLU): creditors hire restructuring advisers; record low the day before earnings
Move: About −13% (≈$2.28) in the Investing.com sidebar (~12:15), after falling as much as ~18% to a record-low $2.16 (Bloomberg headline; Investing.com). Friday's close was $2.63. AP had it −14.5% at 10:30.
Thesis: The selloff has moved from equity weakness to creditor action. A group of senior creditors hired restructuring adviser PJT Partners and law firm Akin Gump. JetBlue's ~$2B of bonds backed by its TrueBlue loyalty program fell to ~42 cents on the dollar from ~80 cents earlier this month (Investing.com). The company must keep at least $750M of quarter-end liquidity and stay below a 62.5% senior-debt-to-collateral ratio; a breach would redirect credit-card cash to debt repayment. Short interest is above 30% of the float, and Q3 results are due tomorrow (consensus loss ≈ $0.55/share). Oil's midday rebound removes the morning's fuel-cost relief. JetBlue says it "remains confident" in its JetForward plan. Into the close this is a binary, event-driven setup: further creditor headlines vs. short covering ahead of the call.
Evidence: Investing.com (10:40 AM; AI-assisted and editor-reviewed): creditor advisers, bond prices, covenants, short interest, estimate. Bloomberg headline (search summary): "JetBlue Shares Drop to Record Low Before Earnings as Debt Concerns Mount." AP via BNN Bloomberg: −14.5% at 10:30. StreetInsider and Simple Flying headlines.
Risks: With short interest above 30%, any liquidity reassurance or financing news on tomorrow's call could spark a violent squeeze. In a restructuring, equity could be heavily diluted or impaired. The ~$2 share price means wide percentage spreads. For peers, AP said UAL (+4.1%) and DAL (+2.9%) were gaining at 10:30 partly on JetBlue's weakness, but that was with oil down ~2%; AAL was only +0.4% at 12:42.
Confidence: Low for direction; volatility likely high
